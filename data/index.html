How a call is made and scored

After a reporting period closes, we register an estimate of what the company has not yet reported. For PNE’s nine months, that was the third quarter, added to the first half it had already published.

An estimate is registered again only if it moves by more than 0.5%. The last version registered before the day of the report is the one scored. A registration on the report day itself does not count, since reports come out before the market opens.

Receipts

Each morning’s vintage is fingerprinted and committed to version control the same morning.

A receipt records when the vintage was written, the code version that wrote it, which companies were published or withheld, and two SHA-256 fingerprints: one of the vintage’s manifest, and one over the list of every file in it with each file’s own fingerprint. Change one number in one file and the second fingerprint no longer matches.

To check a vintage we have sent you, its manifest must give the same fingerprint as the receipt:

sha256sum _manifest.json

Receipts are kept in our version-controlled ledger, one per morning, and are open for audit on request.

Method

Three steps, every morning.

  1. Map each fleet

    From each company’s own reports we record which assets it owns, their capacity, the bidding zone each one sits in and the company’s share. Maps are versioned: an estimate always uses the map as it stood on that day. A company whose map has drifted more than 5% from the capacity it declares is withheld, with the reason recorded, rather than published with a known bias.

  2. Read the grid

    Every morning we take actual generation and installed capacity by zone and production type from the ENTSO-E Transparency Platform. In Great Britain we read the meters of 43 wind farms, 77 units in all, from Elexon’s settlement data, for the four companies with large British fleets. A farm in more than one portfolio, such as London Array, is read once and shared by stake.

  3. Calibrate and seal

    Each estimate is scaled by how the model compared with the company’s own past reports, then written into the morning’s vintage, fingerprinted and committed.

Where meters exist: Ørsted’s UK offshore wind, Q1 2023 to September 2026

A backtest, run after the reports were out: it shows what the method can do, not a sealed record. Read from each farm’s own meters, our estimate came within −0.1% to +1.2% of what Ørsted reported, in every period. Built from zone data alone, it ran 11% to 26% low.

Delivery

Two clocks on every row: the day the electricity was produced, and the moment we could know it.

Rows are only ever added. A revision is a new row with a later timestamp, so any past morning can be rebuilt exactly as it stood. That answers the question every due diligence asks: could you have known this on that date?

event_date
The day the electricity was produced
as_of_ts
The moment we could know it, in UTC
revision_n
0 for the first estimate of that day, then 1, 2 and so on
ticker, isin
The company
zone
The bidding zone
technology
The production type, as grid operators name it
generation_mwh
Estimated generation, in megawatt-hours
coverage_pct
The share of the day’s intervals with grid data
path
Metered, attributed from zone data, or mixed
map_version
The ownership map the estimate used
method_version
The method, so a change of method is never mistaken for a revision of data
source_fingerprints
Fingerprints of the input data
confidence
Verified, estimated or unverified

History

Live, point-in-time vintages since 13 November 2026. History before that is rebuilt with the same code, from 2021 for five companies and from 2023 for the other five. Rebuilt history runs on today’s data, which grid operators may have revised since, so it is a backtest, not a record of what was knowable at the time.

Files

One vintage per morning, as Parquet files, with a manifest that lists the sources the vintage rests on, their licence terms and the reason for any company withheld. The column order is fixed, and a change of method raises the method version.
